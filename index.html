About the model

Each molecular orbital is a linear combination of the valence atomic orbitals of the two atoms, written as Slater-type functions. The calculation solves the secular equations HC = SCE with the overlap matrix S computed exactly for each bond length.

Hii = −(valence-state ionization energy)    Hij = K·Sij·(Hii + Hjj)/2

This is the Wolfsberg–Helmholz approximation used in Hoffmann's extended Hückel method. Because overlap is included, an antibonding orbital is pushed up more than its bonding partner is pulled down. That is why filling both (He2, Be2, Ne2) gives no net bond.

What the model gets right: orbital ordering, nodal structure, bond order, magnetism, and the lopsided orbitals of polar molecules such as CO and HF. What it does not do: predict bond lengths (there is no nuclear repulsion), give bond energies, or treat electron–electron repulsion. Partial charges come out exaggerated. Core electrons are not shown.

One known discrepancy: EHT places the σ2p/π2p crossover between O2 and F2. Photoelectron spectra place it between N2 and O2.

Hartree–Fock mode

The Hartree–Fock option solves the Roothaan equations self-consistently: each electron moves in the average field of the nuclei and all the other electrons. The basis is STO-3G (each Slater orbital fitted by three Gaussians), with core orbitals included. Closed-shell molecules use restricted Hartree–Fock (RHF); molecules with unpaired electrons use restricted open-shell Hartree–Fock (ROHF). Convergence is accelerated with DIIS, starting from a superposition of atomic densities.

Because it gives a total energy, the method can find a bond length and draw a potential energy curve. Optimized STO-3G bond lengths come out within a few hundredths of an ångström (N2 1.134 Å, CO 1.146 Å, HF 0.956 Å). For open-shell molecules the orbital energies shown are α-spin values. Each occupied level then approximates an ionization energy (Koopmans’ theorem).

Limits: electron correlation is missing, so bond energies are too small and some molecules (F2) come out barely bound or unbound. Restricted wavefunctions cannot dissociate bonds correctly, which shows up at the long-distance end of the energy curve. In this minimal basis, F2 comes out with σ2p above π2p, the reverse of the textbook order.

Validation: total energies agree with the independent PyQuante2 program to about 10−7 Eh for every example molecule, and the energies are unchanged when the molecule is rotated off the z axis.
